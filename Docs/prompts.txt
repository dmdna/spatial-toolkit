Spatial Toolkit: App Design Document
Platform: PICO OS (Mixed Reality / Color Passthrough)
Engine: Unity (PICO Unity SDK)
Target Audience: Consumers, homeowners, apartment renters, and DIY beginners.

1. Executive Summary
Spatial Toolkit is an approachable, mixed-reality utility app designed to take the guesswork out of home DIY and furniture shopping. By utilizing PICO's color passthrough and spatial mapping, the app replaces physical tape measures, levels, and painter's tape with intuitive digital overlays. It transforms stressful spatial planning into a fast, visual, and highly forgiving experience.

2. Core Feature Set
Smart Measure: A point-to-point drawing tool. Users click to drop nodes on the physical environment. Displays real-time distance and calculates square footage for enclosed shapes. Includes wall/floor snapping.
Fit Check: A 3D bounding-box generator. Users input Height, Width, and Depth to spawn a translucent, physics-enabled cube representing a piece of furniture. They can drag it around the room to check clearances and walkways.
Picture Hanging Assistant: The user inputs the dimensions of their frames and desired gaps. The app projects a perfectly leveled layout onto the wall, highlighting the exact coordinates where nails should be driven.
Virtual Level & Plumb: A projected laser-level grid that anchors to physical walls. Color-codes (e.g., turns green) when a physical object held up to it is perfectly horizontal or vertical.
AR Snapshots: Instead of complex data exports, users squeeze a button to capture a 2D image of their physical room with all neon measurements and boxes overlaid, saved directly to the PICO gallery for easy smartphone access.
3. UI/UX Design Philosophy
The "Wrist Palette" Concept: The UI is anchored to the user's non-dominant wrist. Rotating the palm upward summons the floating tool menu. This keeps the dominant hand free for pointing and interacting, mimicking a painter holding a palette.
Visual Language: Avoid industrial "construction" aesthetics (black and yellow hazard stripes). Use soft neons, translucent glass materials, and rounded UI corners. It should feel like a modern smartphone app floating in space.
Forgiving Interactions:
Physical Deletion: Grab any measurement node or Fit Check box with the grip button and "throw" it over your shoulder to instantly delete it.
Undo Stack: A dedicated button for instantly undoing the last point placed.
Clear Modality: The controller tip changes color and shape based on the active tool (e.g., a crosshair for measuring, a hand icon for grabbing, a paint droplet for color preview).
4. Control Scheme (PICO Controllers)
The control scheme is designed around a "Pointer (Dominant) and Palette (Non-Dominant)" methodology.

Dominant Hand (The Tool)

Ray Interactor: Casts from the very tip of the controller. Kept extremely thin (0.002 width) with a reticle sphere for pinpoint accuracy against physical walls.
Index Trigger (Squeeze): Primary Action. Drop a measurement node, confirm a selection, or push a UI button.
Grip Button (Hold): Spatial Manipulation. Grab and move Fit Check boxes, drag the entire Picture Hanging layout across the wall, or grab items to throw/delete.
A / X Button (Face Button): Quick Undo. Instantly removes the last placed node or action.
B / Y Button (Face Button): Snapping Toggle. Quickly turn on/off the magnetic snap-to-grid or snap-to-wall features.
Thumbstick (Up/Down): Push/pull the raycast reticle closer or further away when dealing with hard-to-reach corners.
Non-Dominant Hand (The Palette & System)

Palm Up / Wrist Rotation: Summons the Tool Menu (Measure, Fit Check, Level, Settings).
Index Trigger: Secondary action (e.g., lock the Tool Menu in physical space so it detaches from the wrist).
Menu Button: Pause / Exit to PICO Home.
5. Technical Implementation Notes
Spatial Mapping & Collision: Leverage OpenXR environmental raycasting and PICO's scene mesh to generate runtime collision geometry. This ensures Fit Check boxes sit flush on the real floor and don't clip through walls.
Rendering: Use transparent unlit materials for the measurement lines and spatial UI to ensure they pop against the passthrough feed. Single Pass Instanced multiview must be configured correctly to avoid rendering bugs in the headset.
Anchors: Utilize spatial anchors to ensure that if the user takes off the headset and comes back 10 minutes later, their measurements and bounding boxes are locked in the exact same physical position.
